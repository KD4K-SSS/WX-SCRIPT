About the OMISS Propagation Engine

Technical Overview, Mathematical Foundation & User Instructions

What This System Does

Traditional solar indices (SFI, K-index, A-index) provide a global, macro-level estimate of ionospheric conditions, but often fail to predict localized HF propagation for regional nets.

The OMISS Real-Time Band Propagation Guide translates live digital ionospheric probes into actionable predictions specifically calibrated for SSB Voice Communications. It measures real-time path openings from your operating QTH across all HF bands and projects which US states and Canadian provinces are reachable for OMISS net participation.

Data Sources & Accuracy Realities

  • PSKReporter Real-Time Feeds: Uses thousands of live FT8/FT4 reception reports as active ionospheric probes to establish path existence and measure raw signal-to-noise ratios (SNR).
  • Maidenhead Grid & Great-Circle Calculations: Converts 4- and 6-character grid squares into exact geographic coordinates to compute accurate distance radiuses from your station.
  • State & Province Mapping Engine: Maps active grid locator squares directly to US states and Canadian provinces to determine regional availability for net awards.
  • OMISS Net Schedule Feeds: Cross-references live band conditions against active OMISS operating frequencies and net check-in logs.
⚠️ Propagation Realities & Data Limitations:
No HF propagation prediction system is exact. Ionospheric conditions are constantly shifting, and actual contact success depends on antenna gain, local takeoff angles, noise floors, and operator skill. Furthermore, this system is only as good as the live data obtained from PSKReporter—if digital activity is low in a specific region, fewer spots will be reported regardless of band opening status.

How the Calculation Works

Digital modes like FT8 can decode signals well below the human hearing threshold (down to -24 dB). Single Sideband (SSB) voice requires significantly higher signal strength to be intelligible (typically -3 dB or better).

The engine converts digital metrics into estimated SSB voice conditions using the following formula:

Peak Voice SNR = Peak Digital SNR - 14 dB (SSB Bandwidth Penalty) + Power Offset (dB) - Local Noise Penalty (dB)

Key Variables:

  • SSB Bandwidth Penalty (14 dB): Accounts for the wide bandwidth of voice (~2400 Hz) compared to digital modes (~50 Hz), spreading transmitter power across a wider frequency spectrum.
  • Power Offset (dB): Adjusts expectations based on transmitter power relative to a 100W baseline:
    • 100 W = 0 dB (Baseline)
    • 600 W = +7.8 dB
    • 1200 W = +10.8 dB
    • 1500 W = +11.8 dB
  • Local Noise Penalty: Deducts dB based on your local S-meter noise floor (from 0 dB at S0 up to 38 dB at S9+20).
  • Voice Readability Threshold: Paths with an estimated Voice SNR of -3 dB or higher are classified as active SSB paths and populate the active states list.

How to Use the Guide

1 Set Your Location (Precise GPS vs. Grid)

Clicking 📍 Detect My Location triggers a high-precision GPS detection that is exact to your current location, giving you the most accurate radius and path distance calculations possible. Alternatively, you can enter a 4- or 6-character Maidenhead grid square (e.g., EM73) and click Set Grid.

2 Choose Search Radius & Data Sample Density

Select a 150, 250, or 400-mile base radius. Choosing a wider radius expands the geographical area, capturing significantly more active data points. A larger dataset gives a much clearer, statistically reliable indication of overall regional band conditions, especially during periods of light local activity.

3 Select Your Operating Power

Choose your transmitter output power (100W, 600W, 1200W, or 1500W). Increasing power instantly recalculates estimated Voice SNR, expands reachable state listings, and updates path readability status.

4 Set Your Local Noise Floor

Match the dropdown to your receiver's current S-meter noise level. If you have an S7 noise floor, setting it accurately prevents the system from overestimating readable voice paths.

5 Read the Metrics Matrix

  • OPEN: Strong voice signals expected; multiple states reachable.
  • MARGINAL: Readable voice possible with weak signals or QSB.
  • CLOSED: Digital spots exist, but signals are too weak for intelligible voice.
