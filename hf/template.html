WSPR-to-SSB Voice MUF Methodology

Real-time Maximum Usable Frequency (MUF) is traditionally calculated using professional ionosonde radar arrays. This matrix takes a crowdsourced, real-world alternative approach by parsing live packet telemetry from the global WSPR network over the last 15 minutes.

To translate low-power, ultra-weak digital WSPR signals into a reliable estimate for SSB Voice contact, a strict filter is applied via the backend engine:

The -3 dB SNR Filter: WSPR possesses a massive +37 dB processing advantage over standard SSB voice due to its ultra-narrow bandwidth and digital integration. However, stepping up from a 5-Watt beacon to a standard 100-Watt HF transceiver awards you a +13 dB power advantage.

Subtracting that power delta from the digital mode offset means any path reporting a WSPR signal stronger than -3 dB SNR contains enough atmospheric ionization to support a readable, human SSB voice circuit.

The highest frequency band in each Maidenhead grid target meeting this criteria (minimum 4 concurrent spots) is designated as the active Voice MUF limit.
