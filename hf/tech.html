2. Coordinate Mapping & Distance Calculations

Locational telemetry uses Maidenhead Grid Squares (2, 4, or 6 characters). These strings are converted to latitude ($\phi$) and longitude ($\lambda$) centerpoints in decimal degrees:

\begin{aligned} \lambda &= (\text{Char}_0 - 65) \cdot 20 - 180 + (\text{Num}_2 \cdot 2) + (\text{Char}_4 - 65) \cdot \left(\frac{2}{24}\right) + \frac{1}{24} \\ \phi &= (\text{Char}_1 - 65) \cdot 10 - 90 + (\text{Num}_3 \cdot 1) + (\text{Char}_5 - 65) \cdot \left(\frac{1}{24}\right) + \frac{1}{48} \end{aligned}

Distance evaluation between observer coordinates $(\phi_1, \lambda_1)$ and spot endpoints $(\phi_2, \lambda_2)$ is calculated using the Haversine Great-Circle Distance Equation ($R_E = 3958.8 \text{ miles}$):

a = \sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta \phi}{2}\right) + \cos(\phi_1) \cdot \cos(\phi_2) \cdot \sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta \lambda}{2}\right) \\[8pt] d = 2 \cdot R_E \cdot \arctan2\left(\sqrt{a}, \sqrt{1-a}\right)

3. Mathematical Derivation: Digital FT8 to SSB Voice Conversion

Digital weak-signal protocols like FT8 operate in an ultra-narrow bandwidth ($B_{\text{FT8}} \approx 50\text{ Hz}$) with a minimum decoder sensitivity threshold of $\text{SNR}_{\text{FT8, min}} \approx -24\text{ dB}$.

Single Sideband (SSB) Voice uses a standard passband of $B_{\text{SSB}} \approx 2400\text{ Hz}$ and requires an operational minimum threshold of $\text{SNR}_{\text{SSB, min}} \approx -3\text{ dB}$ for readability.

3.1 Bandwidth Noise Floor Penalty ($\Delta \text{SNR}_{\text{BW}}$)

Expanding receiver bandwidth from $50\text{ Hz}$ to $2400\text{ Hz}$ increases the total integrated noise power ($P_N = k T B$). The resulting degradation in signal-to-noise ratio is calculated as:

\Delta \text{SNR}_{\text{BW}} = 10 \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{B_{\text{SSB}}}{B_{\text{FT8}}}\right) = 10 \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{2400}{50}\right) = 10 \cdot \log_{10}(48) \approx 16.81\text{ dB}

Note: In real-world propagation, human cognitive processing and speech processing algorithms allow readable communications at an effective operational penalty of $\approx 14\text{ dB}$.

3.2 Transmit Power Scaling Offset ($\Delta P_{\text{TX}}$)

The baseline PSKReporter FT8 spots are assumed to transmit at a reference digital power level $P_{\text{FT8}} = 25\text{ Watts}$. A voice station operating at power $P_{\text{SSB}}$ gains a power scaling offset:

\Delta P_{\text{TX}} = 10 \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{P_{\text{SSB}}}{25\text{ W}}\right)

3.3 Net Link Budget & Predicted Voice SNR Equation

Combining antenna free-space gain ($G_{\text{Ant}}$), total local noise floor degradation ($N_{\text{Local}}$), bandwidth degradation, power scaling, and the 85th-percentile digital SNR baseline ($\text{SNR}_{\text{P85}}$) yields the overall Voice SNR model:

\text{SNR}_{\text{SSB}} = \text{SNR}_{\text{P85}} + G_{\text{Ant}} - \left(14\text{ dB} - \Delta P_{\text{TX}}\right) - N_{\text{Local}} + \text{Offset}_{\text{Cal}}

4. Statistical Filtering: P85 vs Peak SNR

Taking the single peak digital spot on a band introduces significant errors due to localized ground reflections, anomalous ionospheric focusing, or software decoder clipping (WSJT-X caps outputs near $+12\text{ dB}$ to $+20\text{ dB}$).

To establish an accurate representation of a band's propagation profile, the engine sorts all $N$ valid spot SNRs within radius $R$ in ascending order:

S = [\text{SNR}_0, \text{SNR}_1, \text{SNR}_2, \dots, \text{SNR}_{N-1}], \quad \text{where } \text{SNR}_i \le \text{SNR}_{i+1} \\[8pt] k = \lfloor 0.85 \cdot (N - 1) \rfloor \implies \text{SNR}_{\text{P85}} = S[k]

Using the **85th Percentile (P85)** focuses on the top $15\%$ of active propagation paths while filtering out single-station anomalies.

5. OMISS Data Collection Engine (omiss_engine.py)

The backend engine runs as a system daemon on a 60-second execution loop. It interfaces with OMISS web services to map active nets and check-in rosters.

5.1 Web Scraping Pipeline

  1. Active Net Querying: Requests https://www.omiss.net/Facelift/iPhone/activeNets.php.
  2. URL Parameter Parsing: Extracts the net query parameters from HTML anchor tags, removing NetLogger formatting string masks (e.g., |NETLOGGER2).
  3. Check-In Roster Scraping: Queries checkins.php?net={ENCODED_NAME} to parse active roster items.
  4. Sanitization Filtering: Excludes entries tagged as inactive or out, filters out non-callsign words (e.g., NOTE, OFFICIAL, CLOSED), and enforces callsign regex verification:
    ^[A-Z0-9]{1,3}[0-9][A-Z0-9]{1,4}$
  5. PSKR Correlation: Reads current band status from /var/www/html/data/hf/pskr_engine.json and injects live status flags into the output payload.
  6. Atomic JSON Storage: Writes the resulting structure to omiss_engine.json.tmp before atomically replacing omiss_engine.json to prevent race conditions with front-end web reads.
